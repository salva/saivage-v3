## Design review

Proposal B is still the right architectural direction: deleting `_status`, making persisted state the status source of truth, adding one 5s tick with a re-entrancy lock, deferring F20, and using an injected constructor seam all align with the project guidelines. Approval is blocked by a remaining boundary mismatch.

1. Make the `transitionCard` ownership claim match the actual design surface. The design says every runtime-originated `cardStore.setStatus(...)` or `cardStore.update(id, { status: ... })` goes through `RuntimeStateMachine.transitionCard`, but the detailed replacement story focuses on executor start and planner `updated_cards.status`. Under the architecture-first guideline, keep the stronger design and route all runtime-originating status writes through the machine; otherwise narrow the claim and explain why the remaining direct writes cannot recreate F19/F23-style drift or illegal recovery.

2. Align the `start` versus `restart` rule across the design and plan. The design text says line 706 becomes `currentStatus === 'failed' ? 'restart' : 'start'`, while the plan later uses a startable-state test. The final rule should be based on `STARTABLE_STATES` and `RESTARTABLE_STATES` from [src/permissions/card-permissions.ts](../../../../src/permissions/card-permissions.ts#L28-L50), with explicit tests for `failed`, `done`, `cancelled`, `blocked`, `changed`, `active`, and `running`. This avoids accidentally denying `done`/`cancelled` recovery or treating `active`/`running` as restartable.

3. Keep the constructor seam, but make the staging flag explicit and temporary. The requested seam (`cardStore`, `readState`, `writeState`, `errorLogger`, `clock`, `scheduler`, `redispatchGoal`) is testable. The plan adds `enforceInvariants` for Step 3/Step 4 staging; document it in the constructor contract and require Step 7 to remove it once every construction path passes `true`, so a permanent half-enforced machine cannot survive the cleanup.
